What this is modelling

The 2026–27 Budget announcement describes retaining the discount for pre-transition gains, indexing post-transition cost bases and a minimum tax. This page illustrates that announcement only. The ATO notice, updated 29 June 2026, says the announced changes do not apply to Tax Time 2026 and further resources will be provided.

Hypothetical discount baseline: 50% of the whole nominal gain multiplied by the entered flat rate. The calculator only accepts holdings meeting its calendar 12-month test.

Compound-growth assumption: for a straddling holding, estimated transition value V = C × (S / C)^(pre-years / total-years), where C is cost base and S is proceeds. This assumes constant compound growth, not linear gain apportionment or an observed valuation. It reproduces the Budget Jane example within whole-dollar rounding, but is not a verified prescribed ATO formula.

Calendar-year convention: count whole acquisition anniversaries plus remaining days divided by days to the next anniversary; clamp 29 February to 28 February in non-leap years. Pre-years run from acquisition to transition; post-years run from transition (or later acquisition) to sale. Anniversary-aligned periods are exact whole years. This illustrative, non-prescribed convention is separate from the eligibility test.

Compounded CPI assumption: indexed post-period base B = V × (1 + annual CPI)^post-years. Pre-taxable gain = (V − C) × 50%; post-taxable gain = max(0, S − B). The optional valuation replaces V. For purchases on or after transition V = C; for sales on or before transition all gain remains in the discount portion. CPI is an assumed constant annual rate, not official quarterly index factors.

Minimum-rate assumption: pre-taxable gain × entered rate, plus post-taxable gain × max(entered rate, 30%). Assumes the minimum applies. Means-tested income support exceptions (including eligible pension or JobSeeker payments during the financial year), offsets and interactions are not modelled; no Medicare levy is calculated.

Unsupported: companies, trusts, superannuation, foreign or changing residency, main-residence or other exemptions, new-build and affordable housing concessions, capital losses or loss portions, zero/negative nominal gains, zero cost base, partial disposals, cost-base changes and separately timed cost elements. Acquisitions on or before 21 September 1999 are conservatively excluded, including historical indexation and pre-CGT assets. Short holdings and negative CPI are unsupported. Indexation is floored at zero taxable post-gain here, not used to create a capital loss. Obtain tailored advice for these cases.
