Fast summary

The big distinction is phase: ordinary income and capital gains in accumulation are concessionally taxed, while retirement-phase assets supporting an account-based pension are generally tax-free up to the transfer balance cap.

Accumulation ordinary income 15%

Interest, dividends, distributions, and rent while saving for retirement.

Accumulation long-term CGT 10%

Effective rate after the super fund 1/3 CGT discount.

Pension ordinary income 0%

For assets supporting retirement phase up to the cap.

Division 296 adds extra tax for high total super balances from 2026-27: 15% on earnings attributable to TSB above $3m, plus an additional 10% on earnings attributable to TSB above $10m.

Contribution caps

For FY2026-27, concessional contributions are capped at $32,500 and are generally taxed at 15% in the fund. Non-concessional contributions are capped at $130,000 annually, with bring-forward access based on total super balance immediately before the financial year.

Tax on super balance

These brackets show the tax rate that applies within each balance band and phase, not the cumulative tax across lower bands. Total super balance affects Division 296 exposure; the transfer balance cap affects how much can sit in retirement phase. Ordinary income includes interest, dividends, distributions, and rent. Realised capital gains on assets held for less than 12 months are taxed at the ordinary income rates below; realised capital gains on assets held for 12 months or more use the long-term CGT column.

This deliberately simplifies real life. The 15% / 10% Division 296 rates are additional to ordinary fund tax, so the modelling adds them to the 10% long-term CGT rate for accumulation assets. Actual tax depends on cost base, realised gain amount, personal transfer balance cap, timing, losses, fund expenses, and how Division 296 earnings are assessed.

Fund tax by phase

Ordinary taxed super funds get concessional rates in accumulation and an ordinary income / capital gains exemption for retirement-phase pension assets.

Withdrawal tax from a taxed super fund

Withdrawal tax depends on age, tax-free versus taxable components, and whether the benefit is paid as a lump sum or pension income stream.

Age / condition Lump sum withdrawals Pension / income stream withdrawals
Age 60 or over Taxed element is generally tax-free / non-assessable non-exempt. Taxed element is generally tax-free / non-assessable non-exempt.
Preservation age to 59 Tax-free component is tax-free. Taxable component, taxed element: 0% up to the $260k lifetime low-rate cap; 15% above cap, plus Medicare levy. Tax-free component is tax-free. Taxable component, taxed element: marginal rates with a 15% tax offset.
Under preservation age Tax-free component is tax-free. Taxable component, taxed element: 20% plus Medicare levy. Tax-free component is tax-free. Taxable component, taxed element: marginal rates, generally no 15% offset unless disability super benefit rules apply.

Untaxed schemes, defined benefit pensions, death benefits, temporary residents, and excess contribution releases can have different tax treatment.
